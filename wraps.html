Why wrap instead of paint

Reversible

Peel it later. Factory paint stays underneath if the install is clean and the film is quality. Useful if you lease, collect, or just change your mind.

Faster than a respray

No weeks in a booth for a color you might tire of. Full wraps typically measure in days once film is on hand — scoped in writing first.

Paint stays protected

A wrap is not PPF, but it does take the UV, wash marring, and parking-lot life instead of the clearcoat. Stack PPF on high-impact panels if you want both looks and armor.

Partial is allowed

Roof, hood, accents, chrome delete, or a two-tone. You do not have to wrap every panel to change the stance of the car.

Premium film only

3M and Avery Dennison. Cast film that conforms to curves instead of fighting them. Cheap calendered vinyl is how wraps fail at the edges.

Wrap FAQ

How long does a wrap last?

Typically 5–7 years on quality cast film with sane washing and East Bay sun in mind. Garage-kept cars last longer. We do not sell wrap as permanent paint.

Will it damage the paint?

On sound factory paint, quality film removes cleanly. Resprays, cheap clear, and already-failing edges are the risk — we flag that at inspect.

Wrap or PPF?

Wrap changes color. PPF is clear armor. Many owners do both: color wrap with PPF on the bumper and hood, or clear PPF over factory paint and a wrapped roof.

Can I wrap a lease?

Often yes if removal is scheduled before turn-in and the paint was clean going in. Confirm with the lessor. Reversibility is the point.

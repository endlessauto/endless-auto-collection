## The story across both days

One arc: **Friday is the kick-it, Sunday is the open house, Sunday night is the raffle reveal.** Shoot Friday for texture and faces. Shoot Sunday for scale and the payoff. Cut one recap that uses both.

## Roles (minimum two people)

- **Shooter A, video:** phone or camera on a gimbal. Vertical 9:16 by default, with a few horizontal clips for YouTube.
- **Shooter B, stills and detail:** photos, close-ups, flash or available light. Collects names and permission.
- **Host check-in:** one person tells guests that photos and video are happening. Say it at the door and again before the raffle.

## Shot list

Shoot each shot vertical. Hold every shot 8 to 10 seconds, so editing has room.

### 1. Arrival and exterior (first 30 minutes, before it fills)
- Wide of the building with the lights on, signage in frame. Friday: night. Sunday: golden hour, then repeat at dusk.
- Cars parked out front. Sunday: parking is first come, first served, so a row of guest cars is a free shot.
- Guests walking in, holding dishes. Hands carrying foil trays, pies, bags.
- Door handle, doorway light, first hello. Capture the first-person POV walking in.

### 2. The food (the hero of this event)
- Table wide, before anyone touches it. Then again half empty.
- Close-ups of each dish with the guest's hand placing it. Ask "what is it, who made it?" Get the name on camera.
- Serving spoon pull, steam, a plate being built. Slow motion if you have it.
- The potluck tracker on a phone beside the real table (page to physical, one shot).
- Dessert and drinks as separate beats.

### 3. People
- Candid laughs, not posed. Shoot from across the room with a longer lens or 2x.
- Groups at the table, at the pool table, on couches.
- Three to five one-line interviews, 15 seconds each: "What did you bring?" and "How do you know Endless?" Ask for first name and what they drive or do.
- Team members in the room, not behind the camera. Get at least one clip of each person on the shop team.
- Dress code is **GTA fashion**. Shoot a slow walk-and-turn of the best outfits. This is a Reel on its own.

### 4. The activities
- **Friday, billiards:** a break shot, a ball dropping, a close of the cue chalk, a reaction to a miss. Overhead if you can reach it.
- **Sunday, movies:** screen wide with the crowd silhouetted, then faces lit by the screen. Confirm the film and the setup before you shoot, so the screen is not blown out. TBD.
- **Music:** DJ or playlist screen, a hand adding a song from the Spotify playlist, feet tapping, a crowd reaction to a song.

### 5. The shop itself
- This is an auto shop. Show it. Slow pans past cars, lifts, tools, PPF or wrap work in progress, with people eating in the foreground.
- One shot per service area (PPF, ceramic, tint, wraps, detailing, mechanical) as background to the party. Do not stage a pitch. Let the viewer see the shop is real.
- Any customer cars present: ask the owner first, then shoot detail close-ups.

### 6. The raffle (the payoff)
- The prize table: 1 Month Endless Membership, Full Car Detail or $100 coupon, Endless T-Shirt, plus the free stickers. Flat lay, then a lived-in shot.
- Raffle entry in action: someone follows on Instagram and YouTube, gets a sticker. Close on the sticker.
- The draw: container or screen, hands, the announcement. Shoot the reaction of the winner first, then the announcer.
- Winner holding the prize. Get permission and a name on camera.
- **Sunday is the cleanest raffle shoot.** Plan a camera on a tripod for the draw. Do not rely on handheld.

### 7. Closing shots
- Plates piled up, lights going down, last people laughing.
- A group photo at the end. One horizontal, one vertical.
- A 10-second thank-you to camera from the owner. Warm, plain, no hype. Example: "Thank you for supporting the shop this year."

## Captions and pointers

- Sentence case. No exclamation points. No "insane". Thank the crowd by name when you have permission.
- Always link or reference the next step: follow, RSVP page, or book the shop.
- Do not imply the raffle is a purchase. It is follow-to-enter.
- Photo and video are a private-event concern on Friday. Confirm guests are fine with being posted. Ask at the door.

## Showing Endless as a business while building community

Rule: the party is the content, the shop is the proof. The viewer should feel the community first, then realize the people hosting it are the ones who would work on their car. Never pitch on camera. Per `site-brief.md`: name the specific thing, no "premium" or "world-class", no "don't miss out".

### Seven ways to show the business without selling

1. **Keep the shop in the frame.** Shoot people eating and laughing with a lift, a wrapped car, or a PPF bay behind them. Same shot, two messages.
2. **Show the team as hosts.** Name each person and their role once ("Will, owner", "name, installer") in a caption or a tag. People hire people they have seen being kind.
3. **Let customers speak.** Ask regulars "What did Endless do for your car?" and "Why did you come tonight?" Use their words, their car, the specific job. This is the strongest proof you have. Get permission on camera.
4. **Put work in the background on purpose.** If a finished wrap, tint, or ceramic job is in the room, give it one slow 3-second pass. Caption it plainly: "2023 M2, ceramic coating, [shop name]." Only name jobs you can verify. Mark the rest TBD.
5. **Show the space.** Billiards, movie screen, coffee, sims: a shop with a place to hang out reads as a place that cares. The car meet plan builds on this.
6. **Make the raffle prizes the services.** Shoot the Full Car Detail and the 1 Month Membership as real offers, with a one-line caption saying what each includes. Check the membership page first so you don't misquote.
7. **End on a next step, not a sell.** One soft close per piece: follow, join the next event, or "book a consult" for the people who ask. The metric is leads, not views.

### Caption pattern

Line 1: the human moment. Line 2: the shop fact. Line 3: the next step.

> Forty plates, one pool table, and a sticker for everyone who showed up. We're a PPF, ceramic, tint, and wrap shop in Fremont. Next one is [date TBD]; follow to get the invite.

Check names, numbers, and the date before posting.

### Avoid

- A service-menu overlay on party footage.
- Shooting customers' cars without asking.
- Implying Stillpoint endorsement or any BMW or Mercedes-Benz affiliation.
- Count-based flexing ("200 people showed up!") unless you actually counted.

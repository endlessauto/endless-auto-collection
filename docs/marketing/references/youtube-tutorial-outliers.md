Three promises that win: permanent, cheap, fast. EAC's angle (first timer, sanding explained) is a fourth lane none of them own, but the permanent result still has to be true or viewers distrust it.

## ChrisFix description structure (sample, 2026-10-25)

1. Method name and promise in line one.
2. Contrast with temporary hacks the viewer has already seen, then "my method" in two steps: wet sand, then UV coat to seal.
3. Proof of durability: "1 yr later" and "2 yrs later" update links.
4. Product list with links, one line per item, each tagged by what it does.
5. Cross-links to adjacent videos (the hacks he warns against, moisture fix).
6. Short ask to like and subscribe, with a posting cadence.
7. Social links.
8. Safety and liability disclaimer at the bottom.

## What EAC adopts

- State the two-step method and what makes the result last in the first paragraph, but only claims the video proves. Hedge durability until a follow-up exists.
- Contrast with temporary hacks is a fact, not social comparison. Keep it factual, no naming channels.
- Parts block names what each item does.
- Plan a "1 year later" follow-up. It is proof and a free next video.
- Add a plain-English safety line. House style: no legalese.
- Link adjacent videos from the catalog (full title and URL).

## What EAC skips

- Affiliate links unless the user supplies them.
- All-caps words and double exclamation points in titles.
- "Permanently" or "never turn yellow again" as a claim without a documented follow-up.
- Subscribe begging. Next step is the website or a related video.

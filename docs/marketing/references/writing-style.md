# EAC writing style

Read before drafting any caption, script, title, description, thumbnail text, or archive copy. Voice is precise, transparent, documentation over hard sell. This file lists the exact words and habits to block. Add to it whenever a draft feels off.

## Never use

**Hype words:** insane, crazy, unreal, mind-blowing, game-changer, next level, epic, stunning, jaw-dropping, obsessed, you won't believe, wait for it, absolute unit, hands down, the best.

**Corporate and AI-sounding words:** leverage, delve, elevate, unlock, unleash, seamless, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, world-class, premium (as filler), curated, tapestry, journey (unless a real trip), passion for, dive into, in today's world.

**Pressure and FOMO:** don't miss out, limited spots, last chance, book now before it's gone, you need this.

**Social comparison:** better than the other shops, see what everyone is doing, trending now.

**Punctuation:** em dashes (use a period, comma, or colon), exclamation points, ellipsis for drama (only for in-progress states), all-caps words for emphasis, emoji as bullets.

## Never open with

- "Are you tired of…" or any rhetorical-question opener
- "In this video, we…" or "Welcome back to the channel"
- "Hope this finds you well" or any greeting filler
- "Let's talk about…", "Here's the thing", "Ever wondered…"
- A cliché hook such as "Stop scrolling" or "This changes everything"

Open with the car, the problem, or the result. Name the specific thing in the first sentence.

## Never write

- Claims without a source: "the longest-lasting coating", "the best PPF in the Bay Area". State the film, the warranty, the hours, the result.
- Filler transitions: "without further ado", "so basically", "at the end of the day", "it goes without saying".
- Three-item lists written only for rhythm. List what is true, however many items that is.
- Passive voice. "We installed the film", not "The film was installed."
- Sentences that explain what the next sentence will say.
- Vague intensifiers: very, really, extremely, incredibly, truly.
- Title Case in headings, titles, or buttons. Sentence case only.

## Always

- Real car, real process, real numbers. Unknown means `TBD`.
- One idea per sentence. Short sentences. About 5th-grade reading level for hooks.
- Describe what is shown, not how to feel about it. Mood comes from specifics.
- Numbers under 10 spelled out in narrative copy. Numerals in specs, prices, and timestamps.

## Self-check before showing a draft

1. Search the draft for every item above. Fix each hit.
2. Read the first sentence. If it could open any shop's video, rewrite it.
3. Strike every adjective that a competitor could also claim.

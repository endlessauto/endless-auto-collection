## Competitive analysis: what works for small business origin stories

Three formats consistently generate trust and leads for local service shops. The most reliable for EAC is the **gap story**:

> "I kept seeing [specific problem in the industry]. So I built [the specific fix]."

It works because it does not claim superiority. It explains why the shop exists, and viewers connect with the problem before they consider booking. Two alternative formats — the "one customer who changed everything" and the "before/after life" — both require Will's specific backstory details, which are TBD. Those work well as follow-up pieces once the details are confirmed.

**What almost never works for this format:**
- Opening with "I've always loved cars" — generic, says nothing
- Listing credentials in the first 10 seconds — no one watches a resume
- Claiming to be the best or the only shop in town — collapses authenticity immediately

**Pacing from high-performing origin story Shorts (20-35 seconds):**
- One idea per sentence. No compound ideas.
- Beat every 3-5 seconds. Seven or eight spoken sentences is the ceiling at this length.
- Pauses read as confidence, not dead air.
- End on "who this is for" rather than a pitch. Trust content converts better when the viewer self-selects.

**Word play that moves people:**
- "I write down every job" lands harder than "we document everything"
- "One car at a time. By appointment." lands harder than "personalized attention"
- Naming the city ("Fremont") grounds viewers who are close enough to actually book
- One short declarative sentence, then the specific detail that proves it

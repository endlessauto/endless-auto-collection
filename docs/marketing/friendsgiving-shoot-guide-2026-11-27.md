# Friendsgiving shoot guide: how to record and structure the videos

Companion to `friendsgiving-media-plan-2026-11-27.md`. That file says what to shoot. This one says how to frame it, record it, and build each video.

Fri Nov 27, 8 PM to midnight, Burlingame (private). Sun Nov 29, 4 PM to midnight, Fremont (public).

## 1. Camera setup (do once, both nights)

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Orientation | Vertical 9:16 for everything. Horizontal 16:9 only for the recap long-form, and only if a second camera is free. |
| Resolution and fps | 4K at 30 fps for normal footage. 1080p at 120 fps or 240 fps for slow motion (pours, ball drop, raffle reveal). |
| Exposure | Lock focus and exposure by long-pressing on the subject. Indoors at night, drop exposure slightly so lights don't blow out. |
| Lens | 1x for people and food. 2x for candids from across the room. 0.5x only for the shop wide and the table wide. Never digital zoom past 2x. |
| Audio | Phone mic is fine for room tone. For interviews, clip a lav mic on the guest or hold the phone 12 inches away. Record 10 seconds of room tone at each location. |
| Stabilization | Gimbal or both elbows tucked in. Walk heel to toe. Hold a shot 8 to 10 seconds. |
| Light | Use available light first. A small light only for food close-ups and interviews. Don't use phone flash on people. |
| Storage | Clear 30 GB before Friday. AirDrop or back up before leaving each night. |

Slate every clip: say the name aloud at the start ("Friday, food, lasagna, Maria"). It saves an hour of editing.

## 2. Framing rules

Everything is shot to fit a vertical frame with a safe zone. Keep faces and key objects in the middle 70% of the frame. Platform buttons cover the bottom 20% and the right edge.

| Shot type | Frame | Move | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wide (establishing) | Whole room or building, 0.5x, subject low in frame | Slow push in or still | 6 to 8 s |
| Medium (people) | Waist up, eyes on the upper third | Still or very slow drift | 6 to 8 s |
| Close (detail) | Hands, food, chalk, the sticker. Fill the frame | Still, or shallow rack focus | 3 to 5 s |
| POV | Chest height, walking | Steady walk through a door or along the table | 5 to 8 s |
| Overhead | Directly above the table or pool table | Still | 4 to 6 s |
| Reaction | Tight on the face, 2x from a few feet | Still, start before the moment | 3 to 4 s |

For every moment, get the same action in three sizes: wide, medium, close. That is what makes the edit feel cinematic. If you only have time for one, get the medium.

Keep the shop visible in the background of wides and mediums when you can (a car, a lift, a wrapped panel). Don't block or hide it.

## 3. Interview setup (10 to 15 seconds each)

- Background: shop or a car behind them, not a blank wall. Keep it out of focus.
- Frame: chest up, eyes on the upper third, a little headroom. The guest looks at the person asking, not the lens.
- Distance: camera at eye height, 4 to 5 feet away.
- Ask one question at a time. Ask them to answer in a full sentence that repeats the question ("I brought…").
- Questions, in order:
  1. "Name and what did you bring?"
  2. "How do you know Endless?" (customers: "What did we do for your car?")
  3. "What's your favorite part of tonight?"
- Get a nod yes on camera for posting. Say: "Is it okay if we post this?"
- Don't script their answers. Don't ask them to praise the shop. The specific answer is the proof.

## 4. Run of show: who is where

Times are for Friday. Shift them for Sunday (start at 4 PM, golden hour at the start, dusk exterior later).

| Time | Shooter A (video) | Shooter B (stills and details) |
|---|---|---|
| Doors, first 30 min | Exterior wide, signage, doorway POV, first guests | Food arriving, hands placing dishes, table before anyone touches it |
| Hour 1 | Medium of groups, 3 interviews, activity (billiards or movie) | Close-ups of each dish with the cook's name, prize table |
| Hour 2 | Team shots, shop pans with people in the foreground, GTA fashion walk | Candid portraits, details of drinks, music, sticker table |
| Raffle (tripod) | Locked-off wide of the draw, then handheld close on the winner | Prize close-ups, winner holding the prize |
| Last 30 min | Table half empty, plates, lights down, group photo | Group portrait (vertical and horizontal) |
| Close | Owner thank-you, 10 seconds, standing in the shop | Back up every card |

Raffle: put one phone on a tripod before the draw starts and don't touch it. Start recording one minute early.

## 5. Video structures

Hook rule: the text hook, visual hook, and spoken or sound hook all say the same thing in the first two seconds. Sentence case, no exclamation points.

### A. Teaser, 15 to 20 s (post tonight)
| Sec | Shot | Text |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | Door opening onto the room, sound of the party | "Our shop after hours." |
| 2 to 8 | Three fast cuts: table, pool table, laughing group | none |
| 8 to 14 | Shop pan with people in the foreground | "Endless Auto Collection" (small) |
| 14 to 20 | Owner or group to camera | "Friendsgiving. Sunday at 4 PM in Fremont." |

### B. Potluck table reveal, 20 to 30 s
| Sec | Shot | Text |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | Full table wide | "What a car shop brings to a potluck." |
| 2 to 20 | Four to six dish close-ups with the cook's name | Name and dish |
| 20 to 26 | Half-empty table | none |
| 26 to 30 | Wide, shop behind | "Fremont, CA" |

### C. GTA fashion walk, 15 to 25 s
| Sec | Shot | Text |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | First outfit walking toward the camera, shop behind | "GTA fashion at an auto shop." |
| 2 to 20 | Four to six outfits, walk and turn, same spot, same framing | Name or nickname |
| 20 to 25 | Group pose | none |

Shoot this against one background, in one spot, with the same framing. Consistency is the point.

### D. Raffle draw, 20 to 30 s (Sunday)
| Sec | Shot | Text |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | Winner's face (reaction first) | "Who won the free detail." |
| 2 to 12 | Rewind: prize table, the draw, the announcement | none |
| 12 to 22 | Winner with the prize | Prize and first name |
| 22 to 30 | Next step | "Follow us for the next one." |

### E. Recap, 45 to 60 s (the main piece)
| Sec | Shot | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | Wide of the shop full of people | Hook, "Our first Friendsgiving." |
| 3 to 15 | Arrival, door, food, laughs | Warmth |
| 15 to 30 | Activities: billiards, movie, music | Energy |
| 30 to 40 | One customer line, one team line | Proof |
| 40 to 50 | Raffle draw, winner | Payoff |
| 50 to 60 | Owner thank-you, shop wide | Close and next step |

Music: low, warm, no vocals over speech. Keep real room sound under it.

### F. Long-form, 3 to 5 min (optional, YouTube)
Title seed: "Why we threw a Friendsgiving at our car shop". Cut order: cold open (the best laugh), why we did it (owner), the food, the people (interviews), the raffle, thank-you. Check `youtube-catalog.md` and `publish.md` before locking the title and thumbnail.

## 6. Do and don't

Do:
- Shoot more wides at the start of the night than you think you need.
- Hold every shot. Don't pan unless it is the plan.
- Get at least one clip of every team member and every dish.
- Record the owner thank-you in one take, standing in the shop.

Don't:
- Shoot through a service menu or a pitch. Show the work, don't announce it.
- Film anyone who said no, or any customer's car without asking.
- Imply Stillpoint, BMW or Mercedes-Benz endorsement.
- Claim crowd sizes you didn't count.

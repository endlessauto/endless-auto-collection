# Packaging log

One row per long-form video and one per Short. Its job is to show which packaging change moved reach, so each new video can change exactly one thing.

Source of truth for numbers: YouTube Studio. Views come from `youtube-catalog.md`. CTR and average view duration come from the user on day 4 and day 14. Never estimate them.

## Rules

1. Change one variable per video. Order: thumbnail, then title, then the first 15 seconds. Never title and thumbnail together.
2. Write the hypothesis before publishing, in one line: "Face-led thumbnail will beat the payoff thumbnail on CTR."
3. Day 4: if CTR or average view duration sits below the channel baseline, test the thumbnail first.
4. Day 14: log the result and the winner, then pick the next variable. Low traffic needs the full 14 days.
5. If YouTube Studio offers "Test & compare" on the channel, put up to three thumbnails on the same video. That isolates the thumbnail with the title held constant.
6. Judge against this channel's own baseline: 148 median long-form views, 907 median Shorts (audit 2026-11-12). CTR and average view duration baselines are TBD until Studio numbers arrive.
7. Count on-target results first (comments from car owners, follows, inquiries), not raw views.

## Variables to rotate

| Variable | Versions to try |
|----------|-----------------|
| Thumbnail subject | A face mid-reaction, the payoff object (food, part, car), a group shot |
| Thumbnail text | None, 2 words, 3 to 4 words |
| Title frame | Event plus keyword, payoff-led, named-person moment |
| First 15 seconds | Open on the best moment, open on the place, open on the promise to the viewer |

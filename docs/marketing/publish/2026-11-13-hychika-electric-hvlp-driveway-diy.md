```
A peeling, sun-damaged Lexus RX 330 rear spoiler, repainted at home with a Hychika 600W electric HVLP spray gun. This video shows how we mixed the paint, how the gun sprays, and how we fixed the texture afterward.

We painted it with Spralac single stage from AB Supply in the Bay Area, mixed 4:1:1 (single stage, activator, thinner). No clear coat. The first coats left a lot of texture. We sanded with 1200 to 1500 grit, evened the panel with 2000 then 3000, then cut and polished it. It was not as glass-smooth as the video that inspired this one, but it still got the job done.

The gun lays out paint easily and covers a large area fast. Thinner paint, a firm trigger pull, and wet coats made the biggest difference for us.

Inspired by Triggerman's airless spray gun video: https://youtu.be/XqeKZ7Uw_sE

Whether you've only painted with a rattle can or you're curious if a $65 gun can do real work, this is an honest look from a shop that does paint correction.

TIPS FROM THIS BUILD

• Add thinner. The paint can be too thick for this gun straight from the can. We used 4:1:1 (single stage, activator, thinner).
• Squeeze the trigger harder than you think. Light pressure won't push the paint out.
• Spray wet coats. Let the paint flow out instead of dusting it on.
• Expect texture. We sanded and then cut and polished it out.
• Clean the gun before you put it away. Run thinner through it for about five minutes, until the spray is clear with no color residue. Paint and chemicals can slowly eat away at the seals.
• Use a DA sander. Hand sanding was too inconsistent for us, and you end up re-sanding to remove pigtails and marks from earlier passes.
• Flow rate changes the result. We adjusted ours and saw a difference. We need more hours on the gun to know how much.

Texture tips? Drop them in the comments. If you have run one of these guns, tell us what settings worked.
```

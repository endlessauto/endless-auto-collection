Bilstein B16 Strut Assembly, Front and Rear.

Install Bilstein B16 coilovers on all four corners: take the factory struts apart, preset the new springs, and put it back together at the right ride height.

Vehicle: 2018 BMW M3 F80 Published: Oct 12, 2026 Last edited: Oct 12, 2026

How are your struts?

This car had 168,000 miles on it and was driven daily from Hayward to San Francisco. At that mileage and in that kind of driving, worn suspension is a matter of time.

Symptoms

  • 168,000 miles on the original suspension.
  • Daily commuting, Hayward to San Francisco.
  • The rear struts failed inspection and had problems.

Why Bilstein B16

The inspection only flagged the rears. Instead of replacing them with stock parts, we upgraded all four corners.

Cost. The Bilstein B16 setup ran about $3,600. A comparable KW setup runs about $1,000 more, in the mid $4,000s. The owner chose the B16 on budget, and the performance gap between the two was not big enough to justify the extra $1,000. If you have the money to spend, KW is what we would suggest.

Tools

Two special tools make this job safe. The rest is sockets and a jack.

Special tools

  • Spring compressor. Required to take the old struts apart and to build the new ones. Which one we use
  • Spindle spreader. Releases the front strut from the knuckle and holds it while you swap.
  • Pass-through socket or wrench (18mm). Lets you hold the strut shaft while you remove the top nut.

Sockets and bits

  • Front: 13mm, E18 E-torx, T30, 16mm, 18mm, 22mm, 6mm hex, open-end wrench.
  • Rear: 21mm, 18mm, E12 E-torx, 17mm, T30.
  • Floor jack, rubber mallet, and power tools for the control arm bolts.

Steps

Follow in order. Do the fronts first, then the rears. Lift and secure the car note

Front

  1. Prepare the engine bay

    Lift the hood. If the battery sits near the strut mount bolts, disconnect it.

  2. Loosen the top strut mount bolts

    Loosen the 13mm bolts and the E18 E-torx bolts at the top of the mount. Do not remove them yet.

  3. Move the ABS sensor

    Disconnect it or move it out of the way.

  4. Remove the sway bar end link nut

    Hold the end link with a T30 and loosen the nut with an open-end wrench.

  5. Support the rotor and remove the bottom bracket bolt

    Use a jack to support the rotor. Remove the strut bracket bolt: 18mm on the front side, 16mm on the back.

  6. Spread the spindle

    Twist a spindle spreader 90° to release the strut slightly. Leave the spreader in to hold it.

  7. Remove the strut

    Loosen the top bolts the rest of the way and pull the strut out.

  8. Compress the spring

    Use a spring compressor. Compressed springs store a lot of energy, so work carefully and keep the compressor fully seated.

  9. Remove the strut mount nut

    Use a pass-through socket or wrench (18mm) with a 16mm to hold the shaft. Keep your face away from the top to avoid blowback.

  10. Take the old strut apart

    Remove the strut mount, dust shield and coil spring.

  11. Preset the spring isolators

    Mark the thread so every corner ends up at the same height. Lowering is easier than raising, so start high.

  12. Trim the dust shield

    If the factory dust shield is too long, cut it down three or more notches.

  13. Assemble the Bilstein strut

    Fit the spring, dust shield and strut mount.

  14. Install the strut mount nut

    Depending on the isolator height, use the spring compressor to get the nut started. Hold with a 6mm hex and tighten with a 22mm, then torque.

  15. Install the strut

    Slide the notch into the groove. Watch the cable. Confirm the strut is fully seated before you remove the spindle spreader.

  16. Align the strut mount to the hood

    Hand thread one bolt, check the alignment, then hand thread the rest. Tighten and torque.

  17. Reinstall the bracket bolt

    Tighten and torque.

  18. Reconnect the ABS sensor

    Reconnect it or reroute it the way it came.

  19. Reinstall the end link nut

    Loosen the end link on the other side to help. Tighten and torque.

Rear

  1. Support the control arms

    Use a jack under the rear lower control arms. Loosen the lower control arm bolts (21mm and 18mm). Power tools help.

  2. Remove the rear strut bolts

    Remove the top bolts (E12) and the bottom bolt.

  3. Drop the control arms

    Adjust the jack carefully and remove the loosened control arm bolts. Then lower the jack slowly to make room for the springs.

  4. Remove the rear spring

    Reuse the bottom rubber. Do not reuse the top rubber. Tap the spring mount out of the car with a rubber mallet.

  5. Assemble the Bilstein spring

    Fit the spring with the mount and the lower rubber. Confirm the ride height matches the front.

  6. Install the spring

    Seat it on the control arm with the notch on the bottom rubber facing you.

  7. Move the strut mount to the new strut

    Remove the plastic top cap, then take the mount off the old strut. Fit it on the Bilstein strut, tighten the bolt (17mm and T30), and put the cap back.

  8. Install the strut

    Watch the wire location and routing. Lightly hand thread the top strut bolts.

  9. Raise the control arm

    Jack the rear lower control arm up slowly and insert the new bottom strut bolt and the control arm bolts. This can take a few tries.

  10. Tighten and torque

    Strut top and bottom bolts first, then the control arm bolt.

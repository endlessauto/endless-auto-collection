FORUMS · BMW · 2017 M3 F80

Rear Differential Bushing Replacement.

Replace the worn rubber rear differential bushings on a 2017 BMW M3 (F80) with a polyurethane dual mount kit. Same fitment on the F82, F83, F87 M4 and M2.

Vehicle: 2017 BMW M3 F80

How are your bushings?

The factory rubber bushings crack and tear over time. Once they do, the differential moves more than it should, and you feel it.

Symptoms

  • A clunk when you accelerate or get on the throttle.
  • A clunk when you shift gears.
  • The same clunk when you brake, stop, or roll away from a stop.

Confirm it

Look at them. Inspect the bushings for cracks or rips. A bushing that is torn is no longer usable.

Test for play. Put a crowbar on the rear differential bracket and wiggle the differential. Any play means the bushing is too soft to hold it, and that movement is the clunk you hear.

Revshift Dual Mount Differential Bushing Kit

View the kit on Revshift

  • Polyurethane differential bushings to replace the OEM rubber ones.
  • A second rear bushing.
  • A dual mount bracket that adds a subframe mounting point.
  • Class 10.9 bolts for the rear bushings.
  • Revshift bushing lube.
  • Loctite.

Replace these bolts, do not reuse them

BMW specifies replacing the following whenever they come out.

  • All 4 subframe bolts. Highly recommended.
  • All 4 bolts on the front subframe mount. Highly recommended.
  • The rear diff mount bolt.
  • All bolts on both V braces.

Which durometer

  • 80A Street (black). Slightly stiffer and stronger than OEM rubber, with no additional NVH.
  • 95A Track (green). Significantly stiffer and stronger than OEM rubber, with a minor increase in NVH.

Special tools

The old bushings are pressed in, so you need a way to push them out and the new ones in.

Bushing removal kit

Schwaben silent block tool set from ECS Tuning.

No kit? Make your own

The kit does one job: it pushes the old bushing out into a cup that is bigger than the bushing. If you do not have a large enough metal cup, about 3 inches across for this car, you can make your own puller. We built ours from Lowes and Home Depot parts and used it on this job. You need three pieces:

  • A receiving cup. It must be wider than the bushing, so the old bushing has room to come out into it.
  • A spindle or long bolt. It runs through the middle and does the pulling when you turn it with a wrench.
  • A back piece, the same size as the bushing. It sits against the bushing and does the pushing.

Steps

Follow in order.

  1. Lift and secure the car

  2. Remove the parts in the way

  3. Remove the calipers

    The brake lines limit how far the subframe can drop, so the calipers come off first.

  4. Remove the subframe bolts and install the repair bolts

    Replace the subframe bolts. Do not reuse them.

  5. Support and lower the differential

  6. Remove the old rubber bushings

  7. Install the polyurethane bushings

    Use the bushing lube from the kit on the bushings.

  8. Install the dual mount bracket and rear bushing

    Use the Class 10.9 bolts and the Loctite from the kit.

  9. Reinstall everything and torque to spec

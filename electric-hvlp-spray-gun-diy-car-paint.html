Electric HVLP spray gun: the reality.

We painted a peeling, sun-damaged Lexus RX 330 spoiler with a Hychika electric HVLP gun and no air compressor. Here is the mix, the texture we got, and how we sanded, cut, and polished it.

THE SHORT VERSION

  1. 1. No compressor needed. The gun has its own motor and covers a large area fast.
  2. 2. Thin the paint. Our mix was 4:1:1. Thick paint will not spray well.
  3. 3. Squeeze firmly and spray wet coats.
  4. 4. Expect texture. We sanded, cut, and polished it out. Not glass-smooth, but it did the job.

What we tested

The spoiler came off a Lexus RX 330. The paint was peeling and sun-damaged. We shot it with Spralac single stage from AB Supply in the Bay Area, using the Hychika 600W electric HVLP gun. We did not use a clear coat.

Steps

How we did it, in order.

  1. Sand the spoiler to bare plastic

    The old paint was peeling and sun-damaged, and new paint will not hold on a failing base. We sanded the spoiler down to bare plastic with 220 to 300 grit and removed all of the old paint.

  2. Mix the paint with thinner

    We mixed Spralac single stage, activator, and thinner at 4:1:1. The paint can be too thick for this gun, so the thinner is not optional. If the paint will not flow, check the thinner first.

  3. Spray wet coats

    Hold the trigger firmly. Light pressure does not push the paint through. Lay the paint on wet so it can flow out instead of dusting on.

  4. Clean the gun right away

    Run thinner through the gun for about five minutes, until the spray comes out clear with no color residue, before you put it away. See cleaning and maintenance below.

  5. Sand with 1200 to 1500 grit

    Our coats left a lot of texture. We used an M12 DA sander with 1200 to 1500 grit to take down most of it.

  6. Even the panel with 2000, then 3000 grit

    Step up to 2000, then 3000, to remove the coarser sanding marks and make the surface uniform. Check for pigtails and old sanding lines. Each one means more sanding.

  7. Cut with Rupes D-A Coarse compound

    The compound removes the sanding scratches.

  8. Polish with Meguiar's M205

    M205 Ultra Finishing Polish refines the surface and brings back the gloss.

The texture, and how we fixed it

We had a lot of texture after spraying. Most of it came out with sanding, and the cut and polish finished the job. The spoiler was not as glass-smooth as the airless gun video that inspired us, but it still worked.

A DA sander was the better choice. Hand sanding was too inconsistent for us. You often need extra sanding to remove pigtails and other marks left by earlier sanding.

Sanding is the key step. The cut and polish only bring out what the sanding leaves behind. Expect to spend more time sanding than you think, especially if you want a glass finish.

Cleaning and maintenance

Clean the gun before you put it away. Paint and chemicals can slowly eat away at the seals inside it, and dried paint in the nozzle ruins the next job.

  • Run thinner through the gun for about five minutes. Keep going until the spray comes out clear, with no color residue.
  • Do it right after you spray. Do not leave paint sitting in the cup or the gun overnight.
  • Check the seals. Hychika sells replacement sealing rings, nozzles, and canisters if a part wears out.

Tips

  • Add thinner. Paint straight from the mix can be too thick for this gun. We used 4:1:1.
  • Press the trigger harder. A light pull does not push the paint out.
  • Spray wet coats. Let the paint flow out instead of dusting it on.
  • Test the flow rate. Ours changed the result. We need more hours on the gun to say which setting is best.
  • Clean the gun before you put it away. Run thinner through it for about five minutes, until the spray is clear.
  • Spend the time on sanding. It decides how glossy the finish is after cutting and polishing.
  • Use a DA sander. It is more even than hand sanding and leaves fewer pigtails.

Questions people ask

Do I need an air compressor to paint a car part?

Not with this gun. It is an electric HVLP gun with its own motor, so we sprayed the spoiler with no air compressor and no air hose.

How much texture should I expect?

A lot, in our case. The paint went on with heavy texture. We sanded most of it down and then cut and polished the panel. The finish was not glass-smooth, but it worked.

Why does the paint need thinner?

Single stage paint can be too thick for this gun. We used a 4:1:1 mix of single stage, activator, and thinner. Too little thinner and the gun struggles to put paint out.

Why press the trigger harder?

A light pull does not push the paint out of the gun. A firm, steady squeeze did, in our hands.

Did you clear coat?

No. We used one single stage paint and no clear coat in this video. Primer, base coat, and 2k clear are still on the list to test.

Should I sand by hand or use a DA sander?

We used a DA sander. Hand sanding was too inconsistent for us, and we kept needing extra sanding to remove pigtails and marks from earlier passes.

How do I clean the gun after painting?

Run thinner through it for about five minutes, until the spray comes out clear with no color residue, before you put it away. Paint and chemicals can slowly eat away at the seals in the gun.

Does the flow rate matter?

Yes. We changed the flow rate and the result changed. We need more hours on the gun before we can say what the best setting is.

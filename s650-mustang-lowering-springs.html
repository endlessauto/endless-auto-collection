S650 Mustang GT Lowering Springs Install.

Install Steeda minimum drop lowering springs on a 2024 Ford Mustang GT (S650): the part we bought, the tools, every step, and the torque specs.

Vehicle: 2024 Ford Mustang GT S650 Published: Oct 19, 2026 Last edited: Oct 19, 2026

Why lower it?

Starting point

  • 2024 Ford Mustang GT, S650, with MagneRide.

Why Steeda minimum drop

What Steeda lists. On an S650 coupe, Steeda lists a 0.75 in front drop and a 0.375 in rear drop. Steeda notes the amount can vary by vehicle package and trim. The springs are sold for Mustangs with MagneRide suspension, 2015 to 2026.

Cost. The springs ran $419.95 at the time we bought them.

  • Fitment (Steeda): 2015 to 2026 Mustang EcoBoost and GT with MagneRide.

Tools

A spring compressor and a solid way to support the rear subframe are what make this job safe.

Special tools

  • Spring compressor. Compresses the factory front spring so you can take the strut apart safely. We use the Schwaben strut spring compressor (part no. 013845SCH01A), the same one from our F80 M3 job.
  • Pass-through ratchet, 13/16 in socket, and a long 10mm socket or wrench. The 10mm holds the strut shaft while the pass-through turns the top nut.
  • 17mm open-end wrench. Holds the hex flat behind the sway bar end link while you turn the nut.
  • Clip removal tool. Releases the ABS line harness clips.
  • Heavy hammer or mallet. Taps the splined knuckle bolts out.
  • Caliper hanger hook or wire. Holds the caliper without straining the brake hose.
  • Vice grips, or a towel and clamps. Keep the spring or shaft from spinning during strut disassembly.
  • Torque wrench rated to at least 184 lb-ft. The knuckle bolts are the highest spec on the job.

Sockets and bits

  • Front: 15mm (caliper bolts and strut tower nuts), 17mm and 18mm (sway bar end link), 24mm (knuckle nuts), 13/16 in and 10mm (strut top nut).
  • Rear: 10mm and 13mm (brake line brackets and subframe bracket bolts), 15mm (lower shock bolt), 18mm (upper shock bolts), 21mm and a 21mm swivel socket (main subframe bolts).
  • Support: hydraulic floor jack, jack stands or QuickJacks, and a pole jack or second jack to support the rear subframe and control arms.

Steps

Follow in order.

Front

  1. Lift the car and pull the brakes

    Raise the front so both tires are off the ground and take the wheels off. Unbolt the caliper (two 15mm bolts), hang it with a caliper hook, and remove the rotor.

  2. Disconnect the sway bar end link and ABS harness

    Hold the hex flat with a 17mm wrench and loosen the 18mm end link nut. Both front wheels must be off the ground, or a one-sided load pre-tensions the bar and the nut will not come off. Unclip the ABS harness clips from the strut body.

  3. Remove the knuckle bolts

    Loosen the two 24mm knuckle nuts. Thread each nut on flush with the bolt end before you hit it, so the hammer does not damage the threads when you tap the splined bolt out.

  4. Remove the strut

    Support the strut from below and take off the three 15mm strut tower nuts under the hood.

  5. Compress the spring and take the strut apart

    Mount the strut in the spring compressor. Hold the shaft with a 10mm and back off the 13/16 in top nut with the pass-through ratchet. Move the upper mount bearing, dust boot, and bump stop to the new spring.

  6. Reassemble and torque

    Seat the new spring in the rubber isolators with the coil ends in the indexing notches. Reinstall the strut, then torque the tower nuts to 46 lb-ft and the knuckle bolts to 184 lb-ft.

Rear

  1. Release the brake lines and ABS clips

    Unclip the ABS lines and unbolt the brake line bracket holders (10mm and 13mm) so the lines have slack when the subframe drops.

  2. Support the subframe and remove the shock

    Put a jack under the subframe or knuckle. Take out the upper shock bolts (18mm) and the lower shock bolt (15mm), then remove the shock.

  3. Drop the subframe, one side at a time

    Remove the two 13mm bracket bolts and the 21mm main subframe bolts. Lower the jack slowly until the subframe hinges down far enough to pull the factory spring and rubber isolators. Do one side at a time: dropping both sides lets the subframe shift out of alignment with the chassis.

  4. Install the new spring

    Move the top and bottom isolators to the new spring and butt the coil tips against the isolator grooves. Set the spring in and check that it clears the frame rails.

  5. Raise the subframe and torque

    Jack the subframe back into position and hand-thread the bolts first so they do not cross-thread. Torque the 21mm main bolts to 129 to 140 lb-ft, the 13mm bracket bolts to 40 to 41 lb-ft, and the shock bolts to 66 to 70 lb-ft. Reattach the brake line brackets and ABS clips.

Tips

  • Drop the rear subframe one side at a time. Both sides at once lets it shift out of alignment with the chassis.
  • Lift both front wheels before you touch the end links. Lifting one side pre-loads the sway bar and makes the nut almost impossible to remove.
  • Seat the rear spring in the isolator groove. If a coil touches the metal body or sits out of the groove, it squeaks over bumps. Check the upper rear isolator position before you lower the car.
  • Treat the splined knuckle bolts as one-time use. They are stretch fasteners. A Ford Performance fastener kit gives you clean hardware for reassembly.
  • Get an alignment right after. Lowering shifts camber and toe, and skipping the alignment wears tires early.
